### H2: How to Calculate Your Personal Calorie Needs (Step-by-Step) (~400 words)

#### H3: Step 1 — Calculate Your BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate)
- Explain BMR: calories your body burns at complete rest
- Mifflin-St Jeor Formula (most accurate per research):
  - Men: (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5
  - Women: (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) − 161
- Worked example for a 30-year-old woman, 5'5", 150 lbs

#### H3: Step 2 — Multiply by Your Activity Factor (TDEE)
- Activity multiplier table:
  - Sedentary (desk job): BMR × 1.2
  - Lightly active (1–3 days/week): BMR × 1.375
  - Moderately active (3–5 days/week): BMR × 1.55
  - Very active (6–7 days/week): BMR × 1.725
  - Extremely active (athlete): BMR × 1.9
- Continue the worked example

#### H3: Step 3 — Adjust for Your Goal
- Lose weight: subtract 300–500 calories from TDEE
- Maintain weight: eat at TDEE
- Gain muscle: add 250–500 calories to TDEE
- Mention the "500 calorie rule" (1 lb/week) and link to existing calorie deficit blog post

### H2: 5 Common Mistakes When Estimating Your Calorie Needs (~300 words)
1. **Overestimating activity level** — most people are "lightly active," not "moderately active"
2. **Forgetting liquid calories** — coffee drinks, smoothies, alcohol
3. **Not adjusting as you lose weight** — your needs decrease as you get lighter
4. **Relying on generic "2,000 calorie" labels** — that's a rough population average, not YOUR number
5. **Eyeballing portions** — research shows people underestimate by 30–50%
