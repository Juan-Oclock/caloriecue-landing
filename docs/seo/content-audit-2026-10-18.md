## Priority 1 — Today, Sunday October 18

**Title:** Cronometer vs MyFitnessPal: Which Free Version Should You Choose?

- Slug: `/blog/cronometer-vs-myfitnesspal`
- Primary keyword: `cronometer vs myfitnesspal`
- Secondary targets: `cronometer vs myfitnesspal free`, `myfitnesspal vs cronometer`, `cronometer vs myfitnesspal for protein tracking`.
- Intent: commercial comparison; readers are choosing or replacing their tracker.
- Why now: the free-app hub produces 132 App Store clicks; both adjacent pairwise comparisons already appear in GSC. This fills the missing pair without making another generic roundup.
- Competitive angle: make the free-tier decision concrete using one packaged food, one recurring breakfast, and one homemade recipe. Distinguish viewing protein grams from setting gram-based targets; distinguish free access from a temporary trial. Give a candid recommendation for each user need.
- Quality requirements: re-check current official feature/price sources, date the comparison, and identify market/platform differences. Use actual screenshots and measured tests if available; never label the article tested without completing tests. Disclose that CalorieCue publishes the comparison and give competitors fair recommendations.
- Conversion path: answer the two-app decision first, then show a contextual CalorieCue alternative for readers who prefer photo logging, with an honest review-and-correct workflow and verified plan limits. Measure `app_store_click` by article and placement.
- Internal links: [free-app hub](https://caloriecue.app/blog/best-free-calorie-counter-apps), [Cronometer vs FatSecret](https://caloriecue.app/blog/cronometer-vs-fatsecret), [FatSecret vs MyFitnessPal](https://caloriecue.app/blog/fatsecret-vs-myfitnesspal), [photo-logging workflow](https://caloriecue.app/blog/how-photo-food-logging-works). Add a contextual incoming link from the hub.
- Risk: competitive SERP with established reviewers and vendor content; exact new-keyword volume/KD not verified. Keep the title and H1 on this specific pair to avoid competing for the hub's generic free-app query.
- Recommended brief: quick verdict; dated free-feature matrix; three everyday logging scenarios; decision by user type; where photo logging fits; concise FAQs.

## Priority 2 — Wednesday October 21

**Title:** What Does 30g of Protein Look Like? Food Portions + Free Printable

- Slug: `/blog/what-does-30g-of-protein-look-like`
- Primary keyword: `what does 30g of protein look like`
- Secondary targets: `30 grams of protein examples`, `30g protein portions`, `30g protein vegetarian meals`.
- Intent: visual, practical information with a downloadable reference.
- Why now: combines an established protein cluster (225 Google clicks and 315 GA active users on the ratio article) with a format that already generates App Store clicks (58 on the calorie cheat sheet). This combination is a hypothesis, not an observed result for the new article.
- Competitive angle: original portion photographs plus grams/ounces, calories, preparation state, and food source or specific product label; include mixed-food and vegetarian examples. Show realistic meals, not only isolated powders or huge single-food portions. Explain that 30g protein is not 30g food and is an illustration, not a universal per-meal prescription.
- Quality requirements: verify every portion calculation against authoritative composition data or current labels. Label brand and cooking variation. Do not use an illustrative AI image as proof of a weighed portion. PDF and web table must agree.
- Conversion path: deliver the one-page chart, then show how to log the reader's own meal and check its portion. Track PDF delivery separately from App Store clicks.
- Internal links: [protein ratio chart](https://caloriecue.app/blog/protein-per-calorie), [protein target guide](https://caloriecue.app/blog/how-much-protein-per-day-to-lose-weight), [grocery list](https://caloriecue.app/blog/high-protein-low-calorie-grocery-list), [macro cheat sheet](https://caloriecue.app/blog/macro-tracking-cheat-sheet).
- Risk: medium overlap with existing protein and macro references. Own the exact fixed-protein visual-portion task; do not repeat the ratio rankings, general macro food list, or no-scale methods. Search results confirm visual intent; exact demand and difficulty remain unmeasured.

## Priority 3 — Saturday October 24

**Title:** Should You Weigh Food Raw or Cooked? A Calorie-Tracking Guide

- Slug: `/blog/weigh-food-raw-or-cooked`
- Primary keyword: `weigh food raw or cooked`
- Secondary targets: `raw vs cooked weight calories`, `weigh rice cooked or uncooked`, `raw vs cooked pasta calories`.
- Intent: solve a practical logging error, then apply it in a food tracker.
- Why now: GSC already surfaces cooked-weight/serving questions across chicken and shrimp. Examples: `how much does a cooked chicken breast weigh` (13 impressions, position 10.7), `how much weight does shrimp lose when cooked` (10, position 9.4), `raw vs cooked shrimp weight` (8, position 5.9). These are small signals, not large-volume proof.
- Competitive angle: one decision chart and measured examples for meat, rice, pasta, and a mixed batch. Explain matching the food's weighed state to its database entry. Use measured batch yield rather than a universal raw-to-cooked conversion factor.
- Quality requirements: distinguish water changes from added oil, discarded fat/drippings, and other recipe changes. Check calculations independently and cite reliable composition/cooking-yield sources.
- Conversion path: show the portion/entry review step in CalorieCue and connect to the existing homemade-recipe calculator. Do not imply that a photo reliably determines the original raw weight.
- Internal links: [chicken reference](https://caloriecue.app/blog/chicken-breast-calories-protein), [shrimp reference](https://caloriecue.app/blog/shrimp-calories-protein), [homemade-food calculator](https://caloriecue.app/blog/how-to-calculate-calories-in-homemade-food), [counting guide](https://caloriecue.app/blog/how-to-count-calories).
- Risk: medium-high overlap. Existing chicken/shrimp articles own their nutrition tables, and the homemade-food guide owns recipe calculations. The new page must own the cross-food weighing decision, with short summaries and links. Chicken and shrimp currently produced zero App Store clicks in GA, so this is a lower-confidence conversion experiment than Priority 1.

## Immediate improvements alongside publishing

1. Strengthen contextual links from the free-app and protein hubs to the selected new article. Avoid publishing another generic free-app roundup, protein-ratio chart, or grams-to-calories guide.
2. Investigate lost visits to protein-per-calorie (327 to 225) and the calorie cheat sheet (188 to 143). Evaluate query/device/country mix and snippet performance before rewriting successful URLs.
3. Fix a content-quality error in `content/blog/how-to-count-calories.mdx`: the existing pasta example says cooked pasta logged with dry-pasta calorie density would undercount. At the same logged weight this overcounts. The neighboring chicken example also needs a clearer distinction between actual weight and the selected entry. This is a finding, not an implemented fix.
4. Retain the existing `app_store_click` event and article/placement dimensions. Evaluate unique clickers per article reader after sufficient traffic; assess iOS separately. Do not equate a launch-day spike or an outbound click with a paid install.
5. Ahrefs Health Score 100 is reassuring for the last crawl, but it does not establish complete Google indexation, Core Web Vitals, or backlink quality. Those were not fully audited here.

## Publication gate
