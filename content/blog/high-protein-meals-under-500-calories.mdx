## Breakfast — 5 High Protein Meals Under 500 Calories

These breakfast ideas pair a protein source with fruit, vegetables or grains. Choose a portion and preparation that fit your needs and schedule.

### 1. Veggie Omelet with Whole Wheat Toast

**Approximate calories per serving: 350.**

3 whole eggs, 1 cup mixed vegetables (spinach, bell peppers, onions, mushrooms), 1 slice whole wheat toast, cooking spray instead of oil.

**Portion check:** Count the eggs, bread and cooking fat you actually use. A larger slice of bread or an unmeasured pour of oil changes the total.

### 2. Greek Yogurt Parfait

**Approximate calories per serving: 390.**

1 cup non-fat Greek yogurt (170 cal), ½ cup mixed berries (40 cal), 2 tbsp granola (60 cal), and a scoop of protein powder (120 cal). These example values total **390 calories**. Mix the powder into the yogurt, then add the toppings. Check your yogurt and powder labels; changing the powder or omitting it changes both calories and protein.

### 3. Egg and Avocado Toast

**Approximate calories per serving: 420.**

2 slices whole grain bread, 2 fried eggs (cooking spray), ¼ avocado (mashed), pinch of salt, pepper, and red pepper flakes.

### 4. Protein Oatmeal

**Approximate calories per serving: 419.**

1 cup cooked oats (154 cal), 1 scoop protein powder stirred in (120 cal, 24g protein), 1 tbsp peanut butter (95 cal), ½ sliced banana (50 cal). Stir the protein powder into cooked oats for a thick, pudding-like texture.

### 5. Cottage Cheese and Fruit Bowl

**Approximate calories per serving: 320.**

1 cup low-fat cottage cheese (180 cal, 24g protein), 1 cup mixed berries (80 cal), 1 tbsp honey (60 cal). The listed ingredient estimates total **320 calories**. Choose the portion that fits your day.

## Lunch — 5 High Protein Meals Under 500 Calories

These lunches combine a protein source with vegetables and a measured portion of dressing, grains or bread.

### 6. Grilled Chicken Caesar Salad

**Approximate calories per serving: 457.**

6 oz grilled chicken breast (280 cal, 42g protein), 3 cups romaine lettuce (25 cal), 2 tbsp light Caesar dressing (70 cal), 1 tbsp Parmesan (22 cal), ½ cup croutons (60 cal). Use light dressing — full-fat Caesar can add 180+ calories.

### 7. Turkey and Hummus Wrap

**Approximate calories per serving: 370.**

1 large whole wheat tortilla (130 cal), 5 oz deli turkey breast (150 cal, 30g protein), 2 tbsp hummus (70 cal), spinach, tomato and cucumber (allow about 20 cal for the portion used). Roll tight and slice in half.

### 8. Tuna Salad on Mixed Greens

**Approximate calories per serving: 374.**

1 can (5 oz) tuna in water (120 cal, 28g protein), 1 tbsp mayo (94 cal), diced celery and red onion (about 20 cal), 3 cups mixed greens (20 cal), 1 hard-boiled egg (70 cal, 6g protein), balsamic vinaigrette (50 cal). Tuna is one of the most protein-dense foods per calorie, delivering 28g protein per 120 calories.

### 9. Chicken and Black Bean Bowl

**Approximate calories per serving: 477.**

5 oz grilled chicken (230 cal, 35g protein), ½ cup black beans (114 cal, 8g protein), ½ cup rice (103 cal), salsa (20 cal), 1 tbsp Greek yogurt instead of sour cream (10 cal). Skip the tortilla to keep it under 500.

### 10. Shrimp and Vegetable Stir Fry

**Approximate calories per serving: 463.**

8 oz shrimp (240 cal in this example), 2 cups mixed vegetables (60 cal), **1 tsp sesame oil (40 cal)**, ½ cup cooked rice (103 cal), and soy sauce, garlic and ginger (allow 20 cal). The subtotal is **463 calories**. Cook the shrimp and vegetables with the measured oil, then add the sauce and rice. A tablespoon of oil instead of a teaspoon adds about 80 calories and takes this version above 500.

## Dinner — 5 High Protein Meals Under 500 Calories

Dinner is where most deficits go to die. After a long day of restricted eating, the temptation to overcompensate is strong. The solution: a dinner that feels substantial and satisfying. These meals deliver volume, protein, and flavor without exceeding 500 calories.

### 11. Baked Salmon with Roasted Vegetables

**Approximate calories per serving: 480.**

6 oz salmon fillet (350 cal, 34g protein), 2 cups roasted broccoli and zucchini (80 cal), ½ medium sweet potato (50 cal). Season with lemon, garlic, and herbs. Salmon provides omega-3 fatty acids — [research published in the Journal of Nutritional Biochemistry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29621669/) links omega-3 intake to reduced inflammation and improved body composition during weight loss.

### 12. Chicken Breast Stir Fry

**Approximate calories per serving: 483.**

6 oz cooked chicken breast (280 cal in this example), 2 cups mixed vegetables (60 cal), **1 tsp oil (40 cal)**, ½ cup cooked brown rice (about 103 cal), and soy sauce and garlic. The listed ingredients total **483 calories before sauce**. Measure the sauce and include it in your total; choose a smaller rice portion if needed. Do not add a tablespoon of oil while keeping the original calorie estimate.

### 13. Lean Ground Turkey Tacos

**Approximate calories per serving: 420.**

6 oz 93% lean ground turkey (280 cal, 34g protein), 2 small corn tortillas (100 cal), taco seasoning, lettuce, tomato, salsa (30 cal), 1 tbsp Greek yogurt as sour cream (10 cal). Corn tortillas save ~80 calories per taco versus flour.

### 14. Cod with Quinoa and Asparagus

**Approximate calories per serving: 361.**

6 oz baked cod (180 cal, 36g protein), ½ cup cooked quinoa (111 cal, 4g protein), 8 asparagus spears (30 cal), **1 tsp olive oil (40 cal)**, lemon and herbs. The listed ingredients total **361 calories before seasoning**. Cod is remarkably lean — nearly all protein with minimal fat. One of the best protein-to-calorie ratios of any food.

### 15. Lean Beef and Sweet Potato Plate

**Approximate calories per serving: 422.**

5 oz 93% lean ground beef (250 cal, 30g protein), 1 medium sweet potato (103 cal), 1 cup steamed green beans (35 cal), 1 tsp butter on the sweet potato (34 cal). Season the beef with garlic, onion powder, and paprika.

## How to Build Your Own High Protein Meals Under 500 Calories

You don't need to follow these 20 meals exactly. Use this template to plan a meal, then check its actual calories and protein:

**Step 1: Start with a lean protein (200–300 cal, 30–40g protein)**

Pick one: chicken breast, turkey breast, salmon, cod, shrimp, tuna, lean ground beef, tofu, Greek yogurt, eggs, cottage cheese. For a complete ranked list, see our [high protein, low calorie foods](/blog/high-protein-low-calorie-foods) guide.

**Step 2: Add a vegetable (30–80 cal)**

Pick one or two: broccoli, spinach, mixed greens, asparagus, bell peppers, zucchini, green beans, cauliflower, tomatoes, cucumber. Vegetables add volume; include the portion and any cooking fat in the total.

**Step 3: Add a carb source if calories allow (100–150 cal)**

Pick one: ½ cup rice (103 cal), ½ cup quinoa (111 cal), 1 slice bread (80 cal), ½ medium sweet potato (50 cal), 1 small tortilla (80 cal). Skip this step entirely if you're aiming for lower carbs — the protein and vegetables alone will keep you full.

**Step 4: Add a fat source sparingly (50–120 cal)**

Pick one: 1 tsp olive oil (40 cal), ¼ avocado (60 cal), 1 tbsp nuts (50 cal), 1 tbsp cheese (25 cal). Fat adds flavor and satisfaction, but it's the most calorie-dense macro at 9 calories per gram. Measure carefully.

**The math:** 250 (protein) + 50 (vegetables) + 100 (carb) + 80 (fat) = 480 calories with 35+ grams of protein. Adjust portions up or down based on your specific calorie target.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much protein do I need per meal for weight loss?

There is no single per-meal target for everyone. Start with a daily protein goal appropriate to your body size, activity and health, then distribute it across meals in a way you can sustain. Check the protein in your actual ingredients; the calorie estimates here do not establish a personalized protein target.

### Can I eat too much protein?

More protein is not automatically better. Your meals also need to provide enough energy and a variety of nutrients. If you have kidney disease or a prescribed diet, discuss changes to your protein intake with your clinician or dietitian.

### What if I'm vegetarian or vegan?

Try tofu, tempeh, seitan, beans or lentils in place of meat or fish, and use plant-based yogurt or protein powder where appropriate. These are substitutions, not nutritional equivalents: recalculate calories and protein for the product and portion used.

### Are these meals good for meal prep?

Bowls, cooked proteins and stir-fries can work for meal prep. Refrigerate perishable food within two hours, use refrigerated leftovers within 3–4 days, and freeze portions intended for later. Reheat leftovers to 165°F. Keep cold toppings separate.

### Do I need to eat all 500 calories if the meal is filling?

The 500-calorie cutoff organizes these ideas; it is not a required meal size or a daily calorie prescription. Adjust portions and additions to meet your overall nutrition needs.

For storage details, see the [USDA leftovers guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety) and our [meal prep guide](/blog/meal-prep-for-beginners).
