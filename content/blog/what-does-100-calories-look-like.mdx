**100 calories is about 667g of raw cucumber, 313g of strawberries, 112g of banana, 77g of cooked white rice, 61g of roasted chicken breast, 17g of almonds or 11g of olive oil.** The same number of calories can fill a large bowl or barely cover a spoon.

That gap is the useful part. Once you can picture 100-calorie portions of the foods you eat often, you can estimate a plate faster and see where most of a meal's calories come from.

Below, you can compare 40 everyday foods by weight and household measure, see why the portions differ so much, try four 100-calorie snack combinations, and work out 100 calories for any food from its label.

*Nutrition references checked October 25, 2026 against USDA FoodData Central's SR Legacy dataset. Values are reference averages, not measurements of your food. Household measures are approximate guides; the gram weights are the calculation basis.*

## How many grams is 100 calories?

There is no single answer, because calories come from the protein, carbohydrate, fat and alcohol in a food, not from its total weight. In their pure form, each reaches 100 calories at a different weight:

| Energy source | Calories per gram | Grams for 100 calories |
|---|---:|---:|
| Protein | 4 | 25g |
| Carbohydrate | 4 | 25g |
| Fat | 9 | 11.1g |
| Alcohol | 7 | 14.3g |

Real food is never pure protein or pure fat. It also contains water, fiber and minerals, which add weight but few or no calories. That is why 100 calories of strawberries weighs more than 300g, while 100 calories of almonds weighs 17g. For the full 4-4-9-7 explanation, see our [calories per gram guide](/blog/calories-per-gram).

Each portion below provides about 100 calories for **the listed food only**. Oil, butter, dressing, sauce and toppings are extra. Grams are rounded to the nearest gram and ounces to one decimal place.

Use grams as your reference. Cups and "medium" sizes are rough orientation cues: produce varies in size, and chopped food packs differently in a cup.

## Why 100 calories can weigh 11g or 667g

Three things decide how big a 100-calorie portion looks:

- **Water adds weight and volume without calories.** Cucumber, watermelon and strawberries are mostly water, so their 100-calorie portions are large.
- **Fat packs the most calories into the least space.** At about 9 calories per gram, fat is more than twice as energy-dense as protein or carbohydrate. Oil, butter and nuts reach 100 calories quickly.
- **Fiber adds bulk with fewer usable calories.** Vegetables, fruit, legumes and whole grains contain fiber, which contributes to the size of the portion.

The heaviest portion in this guide (cucumber, 667g) weighs about **60 times** as much as the lightest (olive oil, 11g). That is the idea behind [volume eating](/blog/volume-eating): building meals around foods with low calorie density so your plate looks fuller for the same calories.

Low calorie density is not the same as "better." Nuts, olive oil, salmon and avocado are calorie-dense and can still fit a balanced eating pattern. The chart is a way to see what you are eating, not a ranking of good and bad foods.

## How to work out 100 calories of any food

When your food is not in the chart, you can calculate the portion yourself.

**From a "per 100g" value (USDA database, many international labels):**

**Grams for 100 calories = 10,000 ÷ calories per 100g.**

For example, a food with 250 calories per 100g: 10,000 ÷ 250 = **40g**.

**From a nutrition label serving:**

**Grams for 100 calories = 100 ÷ calories per serving × serving weight in grams.**

Suppose a fictional granola label lists 150 calories per 40g serving. The calculation is 100 ÷ 150 × 40 = **about 27g**. Use the label of the product you actually eat; two granolas with similar names can differ a lot.

There is no need to weigh to the exact gram. Labels are rounded and reference foods are averages. What matters for useful tracking is choosing the right food entry and recording a realistic amount.

## Avoid these 100-calorie portion mistakes

- **Mixing raw and cooked weights.** 77g of cooked rice is about 100 calories; 77g of dry rice is about 280. Weigh food in the state its nutrition entry describes.
- **Counting inedible parts.** The fruit values are for edible flesh. Banana peel, mango pit and apple core are not included.
- **Forgetting the cooking fat.** Roasted vegetables, eggs and chicken are listed plain. Each tablespoon of olive oil adds about 119 calories.
- **Trusting "medium" sizes too much.** One large banana or apple can provide noticeably more than one small one. When it matters, weigh it once and remember the result.
- **Measuring spreads by eye.** A heaped spoon of peanut butter or butter can be much more than a level one. A quick weigh-in for calorie-dense foods improves accuracy more than weighing every vegetable.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many grams is 100 calories?

It depends on the food. Pure protein or carbohydrate provides 100 calories in 25g, pure fat in about 11g and alcohol in about 14g. In whole foods, the range in this guide runs from about 11g of olive oil to about 667g of raw cucumber.

### What does 100 calories of fruit look like?

About 313g of strawberries (roughly 2 cups halved), 175g of blueberries, 145g of grapes (about 30 grapes), 192g of apple (one medium-large apple) or 112g of peeled banana (just under one medium banana).

### What is a good 100-calorie snack?

Try 100g plain nonfat Greek yogurt with 70g blueberries, one large hard-boiled egg with 150g cucumber, 70g 1% cottage cheese with 155g strawberries, or 90g apple with 9g peanut butter. Whether 100 calories is enough depends on your appetite and daily plan.

### Why is 100 calories of oil so small?

Oil is almost entirely fat, at about 9 calories per gram, with almost no water. 100 calories of olive oil is about 11g, or three-quarters of a tablespoon. A full tablespoon is about 119 calories.

### Should I weigh food raw or cooked for these portions?

Match the state shown in the chart. Rice, pasta, legumes, meat, fish, potatoes and eggs use cooked weights; oats use dry weight; fruit and most vegetables are raw.

### How do I work out 100 calories from a nutrition label?

Divide 100 by the calories per serving, then multiply by the serving weight in grams. For a label listing 150 calories per 40g serving, 100 ÷ 150 × 40 = about 27g.
