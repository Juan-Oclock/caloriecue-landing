**FatSecret and Cronometer are our starting recommendations for free calorie tracking with barcode scanning.** Cronometer is the stronger fit if you want detailed nutrients and reusable homemade recipes. FatSecret is worth trying if you want a food diary with community support.

MyFitnessPal and Lose It! also have ongoing free plans, but the exact task matters: viewing protein grams is different from setting a custom gram goal, and entering a recipe manually is different from importing it automatically.

## What "Free" Actually Means in a Calorie App

Compare three separate offers:

- **Free download:** you can install the app without paying. This does not establish which features you can keep using.
- **Ongoing free plan:** specified features remain available without starting a subscription. It can coexist with paid upgrades.
- **Premium trial:** paid features are available temporarily under the trial's eligibility and renewal terms.

FatSecret, Cronometer, MyFitnessPal, and Lose It! all offer free and paid features.

Before choosing, name the tasks you need: food search, scanning, protein intake, saving recipes, and repeating a meal. Then check those tasks against the free plan instead of relying on the word “free” on the download button.

## 1. FatSecret — Free Barcode Scanning and Community

FatSecret's [official app page](https://www.fatsecret.com/app) lists free macro tracking, barcode scanning, and community support. It belongs on your shortlist if you want to log packaged foods without paying for a scanner.

The limitation is in the shortcuts. **Premium includes Custom Meals, Copy Food, one-tap recipe logging, and AI logging features.** Free food tracking does not mean every way of saving or repeating a meal is free. [FatSecret Premium comparison](https://www.fatsecret.com/premium).

Try the free diary with the foods you buy most often. Check the matched product and serving size against its label. A scanner can identify a product, but it does not measure the portion you ate.

**Choose it when:** free scanning and community features are useful to you. Check recipe and repeat-meal shortcuts before committing your meal-prep routine to the app.

For a closer comparison, read [FatSecret vs MyFitnessPal](/blog/fatsecret-vs-myfitnesspal) or [Cronometer vs FatSecret](/blog/cronometer-vs-fatsecret).

## 2. Cronometer — Detailed Nutrients and Homemade Recipes

Cronometer Basic includes barcode scanning, macro and micronutrient tracking, and custom targets. We recommend it when you want to look beyond calorie totals and review more detailed nutrition information. [Cronometer Basic and Gold](https://cronometer.com/gold/).

**Manual custom recipes and meals are free.** You can build a reusable entry from ingredients without buying Gold. Automatic importing from a recipe URL is a separate paid convenience. [Custom meals and recipes](https://cronometer.com/features/custom-meals-and-recipes.html) and [Recipe Importer](https://cronometer.com/blog/recipe-importer/).

For someone who cooks a batch and eats different portions over several days, saving the ingredients and getting the serving definition right may matter more than how quickly the first food search appears.

More nutrient detail does not establish that your particular log is more accurate. You still need to choose the matching food and enter its amount. We have not run a controlled accuracy comparison between these apps.

**Choose it when:** you want detailed nutrient information or reusable homemade recipes. Our [Cronometer vs FatSecret comparison](/blog/cronometer-vs-fatsecret) explains the differences in copying meals and paid shortcuts.

## 3. Lose It! — A Free Calorie Budget

Lose It! Basic provides a personalized daily calorie budget and food-database access. That makes it an option for someone who wants to begin with a calorie diary and evaluate additional features later. [Lose It! membership tiers](https://loseit.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/51906523474708-Lose-It-Membership-Tiers-Pricing).

Its official plan lists **barcode scanning, custom nutrient targets, and smart photo and voice logging under Premium**. However, its barcode help page says some free members retain scanner access during a transition, and another Lose It! page describes scanning as free. Check your own account instead of assuming it is included. [Barcode access help](https://loseit.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/47773984823700-Unlocking-the-Barcode-Scanner-Scan-It).

A free calorie budget may be sufficient for your routine. If you specifically need a free scanner, begin with FatSecret or Cronometer instead of assuming you must subscribe.

**Choose it when:** you want to try its calorie-budget workflow and are comfortable with the documented free features.

## 4. MyFitnessPal — Saved Meals and an Established Routine

MyFitnessPal's free plan includes manual food logging and creating foods, meals, and recipes. **You can view macro intake in grams for free.** Its free macro goals use percentages in five-percentage-point increments. [MyFitnessPal free features](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/15457546881805-What-is-included-in-the-free-version).

The paid distinction is setting custom macro goals directly in grams. The barcode scanner also requires Premium according to its current documentation. [Premium features](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032625951-MyFitnessPal-Premium-features) and [barcode-scanner policy](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032624771-How-to-use-the-Barcode-Scanner).

That difference matters if you want an exact protein target displayed in the app. It does not prevent you from seeing how much protein you logged.

If you already have a useful collection of checked recipes and saved meals, rebuilding them elsewhere takes time. Stay when the free workflow meets your needs; switch when another app solves a recurring limitation.

**Choose it when:** familiar saved meals or an existing diary make logging easier. Read [FatSecret vs MyFitnessPal](/blog/fatsecret-vs-myfitnesspal) before switching solely for free barcode access.

## 5. Samsung Health and Apple Health — Different Roles

These should not be treated as identical food trackers.

**Samsung Health has a food tracker.** Samsung announced barcode scanning with FatSecret nutrition data, initially in the U.S. and selected European markets. Check availability on your device and in your region. [Samsung's food-tracking announcement](https://news.samsung.com/us/samsung-health-app-update-makes-accessing-health-records-managing-medications-food-tracking-easier/).

**Apple Health brings health information from compatible apps and devices into one place**, and supports manual data entry. Treat it as a place to review and manage shared data; check the connected food app's logging features separately. [Apple's Health-data guide](https://support.apple.com/en-asia/108779).

Review sharing permissions when connecting any tracker. We do not make blanket claims here that every connected app keeps all food information only on your device or has identical privacy practices.

**Choose this route when:** you already use the health platform and its available food-tracking setup covers your needs. Verify the actual logging workflow before treating it as a replacement for a dedicated diary.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is MyFitnessPal still free?

Yes. Free users can log food, view macro intake in grams, and create meals and recipes. Barcode scanning and custom macro goals set directly in grams are paid features.

### What's the most accurate free calorie tracker?

We have not established an accuracy winner. Cronometer is our recommendation for detailed nutrient information. Correct food selection and portion entry matter in every app.

### Are any AI photo calorie trackers free?

CalorieCue includes 3 meal-photo scans and 3 AI Coach messages per day on its ongoing free plan. Review estimates before saving. Premium and its optional trial are separate offers.

### Is FatSecret completely free?

It has a free plan and optional Premium. Some meal shortcuts and AI logging features cost extra.

### Does Lose It! include free barcode scanning?

Lose It! lists scanning under Premium, but says some free members retain access during a transition. Check the feature in your own account.

### Do I need to pay for a calorie tracker?

You can record calories and macros on a free plan. Pay only when a specific feature is useful enough to you to justify its cost; a subscription alone does not establish better results.
