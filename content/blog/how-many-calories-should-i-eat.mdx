**Your daily calorie target is an estimate to test, not a number determined by age alone.** Start with your resting energy needs, account for activity, then adjust for your goal. The [TDEE calculator](/tdee-calculator) does the arithmetic; this guide explains how to interpret the result.

The 2,000-calorie reference on food labels is not a prescription for everyone. Two people of the same age can need different amounts because of body size, activity, health and other factors.

<Callout type="info">
This guide is for adults planning everyday meals. Children, pregnant or breastfeeding people, and people managing an eating disorder or a condition that affects nutrition need individualized advice. The [NIH Body Weight Planner](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/body-weight-planner) also excludes children and pregnancy/breastfeeding from its general adult calculations.
</Callout>

## How to Calculate Your Personal Calorie Needs

### Step 1 — Calculate Your BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate)

A common starting point is the [Mifflin–St Jeor equation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/?dopt=Abstract). It predicts resting energy expenditure; calculators often label this estimate BMR. It does not measure your metabolism directly.

Using weight in kilograms, height in centimetres and age in years:

- **Male equation:** (10 × weight) + (6.25 × height) − (5 × age) + 5
- **Female equation:** (10 × weight) + (6.25 × height) − (5 × age) − 161

These sex-specific equations are population estimates. Body composition and individual circumstances can make the estimate less representative.

**Worked example:** For a 30-year-old woman weighing 68 kg and measuring 165 cm:

(10 × 68) + (6.25 × 165) − (5 × 30) − 161 = **1,400.25 calories per day**, rounded to 1,400.

That is estimated resting expenditure, not a recommended food intake.

### Step 2 — Multiply by Your Activity Factor (TDEE)

Total daily energy expenditure includes activity as well as resting needs. Common calculator multipliers are practical approximations:

| Activity pattern | Approximate multiplier |
|---|---:|
| Mostly seated, little exercise | 1.2 |
| Light activity | 1.375 |
| Moderate activity | 1.55 |
| Very active | 1.725 |
| Extremely active | 1.9 |

Exercise frequency alone does not determine the category. Work, walking, training duration and recovery all affect the result. Avoid automatically choosing a high multiplier because you go to the gym.

At a multiplier of 1.55, the example above gives **1,400 × 1.55 = 2,170 calories per day**. Treat this as a starting maintenance estimate. The [TDEE guide](/blog/tdee-calculator-guide) explains the calculator inputs in more detail.

### Step 3 — Adjust for Your Goal

For illustration, here is how different choices change that same estimate:

| Goal or adjustment | Calculation | Example daily intake |
|---|---|---:|
| Maintenance starting point | 2,170 | 2,170 |
| Smaller deficit | 2,170 − 300 | 1,870 |
| Larger deficit | 2,170 − 500 | 1,670 |
| Modest surplus example | 2,170 + 200 | 2,370 |

These are arithmetic examples, not targets for every reader. A deficit of the same size can be manageable for one person and excessive for another. For the next step, use the [calorie deficit guide](/blog/how-to-calculate-calorie-deficit).

## Calorie Needs for Common Goals

### How Many Calories to Lose Weight

If weight loss is appropriate for you, choose an intake you can maintain while eating varied meals and supporting daily activity. A calculator cannot guarantee a weekly loss rate. The [CDC's weight-loss guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/losing-weight/index.html) favors gradual change and recognizes that medicines, conditions, stress, hormones and age can affect weight management.

Avoid treating 1,200 or 1,500 calories as a universally safe minimum. Persistent fatigue, dizziness, difficulty recovering from exercise or a worsening relationship with food are reasons to reassess with a healthcare professional.

### How Many Calories to Build Muscle

A calorie target is only one part of a muscle-building plan. Resistance training, sufficient protein and recovery matter too. Begin with an estimate, track performance and progress, and adjust food intake gradually rather than assuming a large surplus is necessary. Our [protein-per-day guide](/blog/how-much-protein-per-day-to-lose-weight) explains how protein targets depend on context.

### How Many Calories to Maintain Weight

Maintenance is a range, not an exact daily quota. Use a similar routine for several weeks and look at the trend. If weight is reasonably stable and energy and training feel appropriate, your intake is probably close to your current needs.

## Check Your Estimate Against Real Life

1. **Keep a usable record.** Include drinks, sauces and cooking ingredients, not just the main item on the plate.
2. **Compare like with like.** If weighing yourself is helpful, use similar conditions and compare weekly averages rather than individual readings.
3. **Allow for water changes.** Sodium, carbohydrate intake, digestion and menstrual-cycle changes can move the scale without equivalent changes in fat. Read [why weight fluctuates daily](/blog/why-your-weight-fluctuates-daily) before changing your target after one weigh-in.
4. **Review energy and recovery.** Hunger, concentration and exercise performance matter alongside weight.
5. **Adjust gradually if needed.** If the trend stays off course, review portions and activity assumptions first. Our [plateau guide](/blog/how-to-break-weight-loss-plateau) helps distinguish a stall from normal variation.

## 5 Common Mistakes When Estimating Your Calorie Needs

- **Treating an estimate as a measurement.** Neither an equation nor an activity tracker directly measures everything you burn.
- **Counting exercise twice.** If your activity multiplier already includes your usual workouts, adding the same workout calories again changes the intended target.
- **Mixing raw and cooked weights.** Choose a database entry that matches how the food was weighed. Water changes the weight and therefore the calories per gram.
- **Missing oils and drinks.** A meal photo cannot show every ingredient. Record additions you know about.
- **Reacting to one day.** A salty dinner can change tomorrow's weight without requiring a new calorie target. Changes in body size over time may justify recalculating; daily noise usually does not.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many calories should a 40-year-old woman eat per day?

Age alone cannot set a calorie target. Height, weight, activity and goals also matter. Estimate resting needs, apply an activity factor, then compare the estimate with your energy levels and weight trend over several weeks.

### Is 1,500 calories a day enough to lose weight?

It may create a deficit for some adults, but it is not a universal target or a guarantee of a particular loss rate. Choose an intake that fits your estimated needs, health and activity rather than copying a fixed number.

### Should I eat back the calories I burn exercising?

First check whether your daily target already includes your usual exercise. Adding those calories again can double-count activity. There is no universal percentage to eat back; longer or harder training may require an individualized fueling plan.

### How many calories should I eat while breastfeeding?

CDC guidance recommends about 330–400 additional calories per day for well-nourished breastfeeding mothers compared with intake before pregnancy. Individual needs depend on activity, body size and feeding pattern. Discuss calorie targets with your healthcare professional.

### Do I need to count calories forever?

No. Tracking can be a temporary way to understand portions and patterns. You can use repeatable meals or occasional check-ins instead. If tracking causes distress or restrictive eating, seek support rather than tightening the target.

The breastfeeding estimate above comes from the [CDC's maternal diet guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/breastfeeding-special-circumstances/hcp/diet-micronutrients/maternal-diet.html).

## Your Next Step

Calculate a starting estimate, choose meals you can repeat, and review several weeks of real-life feedback. You do not need an exact metabolism number to start building a useful routine.
