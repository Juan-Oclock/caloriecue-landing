## Cronometer vs FatSecret at a glance

Both have an ongoing free plan and optional subscriptions. Here are the differences most relevant to someone who wants to stay on the free plan.

| Task | Cronometer | FatSecret |
|---|---|---|
| Track calories and macros | Free | Free |
| Scan packaged-food barcodes | Free | Free |
| Save custom meals | Free | Premium |
| Copy previous food entries | Free copy/paste | Premium Copy Food |
| Log a meal from a photo | Gold Photo Log | Premium Smart Food Scan |

Sources: [Cronometer's plan comparison](https://cronometer.com/gold/), [its food-logging shortcuts](https://cronometer.com/blog/log-food-fast/), [FatSecret's free features](https://www.fatsecret.com/app), and [FatSecret's Premium comparison](https://www.fatsecret.com/premium).

Meal-saving features need some care. Recording the ingredients in a meal, saving a reusable combination, importing an online recipe, and automatically scheduling a repeat meal are separate functions. An app can offer one free and charge for another.

## For protein tracking, both free plans cover the basics

If your question is simply “How much protein have I logged today?”, either app belongs on your shortlist. Free macro tracking includes protein alongside carbohydrate and fat.

Cronometer explicitly includes **custom macro and micronutrient targets** in Basic. Those controls are relevant if you want to inspect more than calories and protein in the same diary. [Cronometer Basic features](https://cronometer.com/gold/).

FatSecret's free app page includes macro tracking, but that alone does not establish that all target-setting controls match Cronometer's. During setup, check whether the particular goal you want can be entered and displayed the way you expect. [FatSecret app features](https://www.fatsecret.com/app).

Keep three tasks separate:

1. **Viewing intake:** reading the protein grams already logged.
2. **Setting a goal:** deciding which target the app compares those grams against.
3. **Choosing food:** finding a meal that fits the rest of your day.

An app can help with the first two without making the third decision for you. Our [protein-per-calorie guide](/blog/protein-per-calorie) helps compare foods, while the [calories-per-gram explainer](/blog/calories-per-gram) covers how macros contribute to calories.

## For saved homemade recipes, compare the free shortcuts

**If you know the ingredients and batch yield, a saved recipe can make repeat logging useful.** Cronometer's free custom-meal and recipe tools let you build reusable entries from ingredients. You can enter a recipe manually without paying for automatic web importing. [Cronometer's custom meals and recipes](https://cronometer.com/features/custom-meals-and-recipes.html).

Consider a pot of chili. The useful workflow is to record the ingredients once, include the cooking oil, set the batch yield, and log the portion you serve. Tomorrow, you want to reuse that checked recipe with a different portion size.

For an illustrative batch totaling 1,800 calories and weighing 1,500 grams after cooking:

- Each gram represents **1.2 calories**: 1,800 ÷ 1,500.
- A 350-gram portion represents **420 calories**: 350 × 1.2.
- Calling every bowl “one serving” only works if the bowls match the serving definition you created.

Those are invented numbers to demonstrate the workflow, not nutrition data for a specific chili. Our [homemade-food calorie guide](/blog/how-to-calculate-calories-in-homemade-food) includes a calculator for your own ingredients and batch.

Cronometer's **Recipe Importer** is different: Gold users can paste a recipe URL to bring in ingredients automatically, then review the matches. You are paying for a shortcut to entry, not for the basic ability to create a recipe. [Cronometer Recipe Importer](https://cronometer.com/blog/recipe-importer/).

FatSecret places **Custom Meals and one-tap recipe logging** in Premium. Its free diary still lets you track food, but check the exact save-and-reuse sequence you need before building your meal-prep routine around it. [FatSecret plan details](https://www.fatsecret.com/premium).

## For repeat breakfasts, test the shortcut before choosing

A useful food diary should make your second week easier than your first. If breakfast is usually the same oats, milk, and fruit, you should not need to reconsider every ingredient each morning.

Cronometer documents copy-and-paste food entries among its free logging shortcuts. Its automatic **Repeat Items** scheduling is Gold, so manually copying yesterday's breakfast and having breakfast appear automatically are different features. [Cronometer logging shortcuts](https://cronometer.com/blog/log-food-fast/) and [Repeat Items guide](https://cronometer.com/blog/repeating-food/).

FatSecret's help page states that **Copy Food requires Premium**. That matters more to a repeat-meal user than a long list of foods in a database. [FatSecret Copy Food guide](https://www.fatsecret.com/fatsecret-app-help/getting-started-with-premium/how-to-copy-food).

Our recommendation: try repeating your usual breakfast while staying on each app's free plan. Note the number of corrections you need and whether the shortcut you want triggers an upgrade. Choose around that repeated task, not the welcome screen.

## Barcode scanning is a tie; portion checks still matter

Both free plans list a barcode scanner. It is useful for finding the right packaged product, but the barcode does not tell either app how much you ate.

After scanning, check:

- The product, flavor, and package match.
- The nutrition entry uses the same unit as the label.
- Your logged amount reflects what you ate, including multiple servings.

For example, if a package contains two servings and you eat all of it, logging one serving records half the package's listed calories. Switching apps will not fix that quantity error.

Regional products also deserve a trial. Scan a few foods you actually buy, and check whether you can correct or create an entry when the match is missing. Neither this comparison nor a database-size claim establishes how well a particular app covers your shopping basket.

## When FatSecret's community fits your routine

**If sharing progress is part of your routine, consider the community features alongside the diary.** FatSecret's official app page includes community support in the free experience. [FatSecret's free app](https://www.fatsecret.com/app).

If you already have checked food entries and a diary you use comfortably, rebuilding them has a cost in time and attention. Extra nutrient detail only helps when it serves a need you actually have.

The reason to switch is a recurring limitation: perhaps you want reusable recipes without a subscription, or you need a nutrient view your current setup does not provide. Keep a personal record of important recipes and targets before moving; we have not established an automatic transfer of diary history between these apps.
