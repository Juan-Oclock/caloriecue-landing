Start with the basics

Calorie counting helps you notice patterns in what you eat. The goal is to make informed choices that fit your life.

What is a calorie?

A calorie is a unit of energy. Your body uses energy throughout the day, including at rest.

How it works

1

Be aware — Notice meals and portions.

2

Keep it simple — Choose a manageable method.

3

Look at the big picture — Focus on habits over time.

Practical tip

Start by noticing a typical day of eating.

Find your

Your calorie needs depend on your body, activity and health. A calculator offers an estimate, not a prescription.

1

Estimate — Use the CalorieCue TDEE calculator if a number is useful.

2

3

Adjust — Seek individualized advice when needed.

Try one ordinary meal

Log each part of a familiar lunch: 1 cup cooked rice, 4 oz cooked chicken, and any cooking oil or sauce. Match your food entry to the way you weighed it: raw or cooked.

Practical tip

Avoid judging progress from a single day.

General education for adults. Seek professional advice for pregnancy, medical conditions or a history of disordered eating.

Check the serving first

The label describes one serving. Compare that with the amount you eat.

2 servings × 200 calories = 400 calories
Match your entry

A restaurant estimate

If the menu lists a bowl at 650 calories, then a sauce at 80 and a drink at 120, log all three: 650 + 80 + 120 = 850 calories. These are example numbers; use the restaurant’s figures when available.

Practical tip

One meal does not define your progress.

Make tomorrow easier

If breakfast is usually yogurt, oats and berries, save it as a meal in your tracker. Reuse the entry and adjust the portions each time. A useful routine can stay simple.

Practical tip

If tracking becomes stressful or rigid, take a break and seek support from a qualified professional.
