# CalorieCue Google Search Console audit

Audited September 7, 2026 using the signed-in Chrome session for `sc-domain:caloriecue.app`, live article pages, and the current local `main` source. Page-indexing report last updated September 4. Performance is Web search.

## Assessment

The site does not show a broad technical indexing failure. Google reports 53 indexed URLs and 29 excluded URLs. Of the exclusions, 11 are expected redirect/canonical/noindex cases, six are legacy www URLs, and one is an old redirect error that now passes Google's live test. The remaining 11 are nine crawled articles, one discovered article, and the terms page. Prioritize the ten articles; indexing legal boilerplate is a lower business priority.

Google does not disclose the precise selection reason for each crawled-but-unindexed article. Content trust, distinctiveness, internal linking, and recrawl freshness are evidence-backed improvement areas, not proven algorithmic causes.

No website changes, indexing requests, validation submissions, or GSC settings changes were made. Google live tests were run for the old redirect and the daily-calorie article.

### What needs attention

1. **High visibility, low click-through:** `calories-in-food-list` has 71,835 impressions and 95 clicks (0.1% rounded CTR), position 11.7. `calories-per-gram` has 69,822 impressions and 79 clicks (0.1%), position 7.7. Review query intent and search-result presentation before changing titles. Clear charts, serving units, tools and downloadable resources can provide reasons to visit. Simple-answer searches may not produce many clicks; no SERP-feature cause was established here.
2. **Protein-per-calorie is slipping:** 298 → 275 clicks despite impressions increasing 34,069 → 40,777. CTR declined 0.9% → 0.7%. The specific query “protein to calorie ratio” fell 21 → 7 clicks and position 5.1 → 8.1. Prioritize its formula, worked examples and ranking table.
3. **Aggregate impression decline is concentrated:** `how-to-track-calories` fell 184,362 → 12,885 impressions (−171,477), but stayed at eight clicks. This single page's fall exceeds the site's net decline; other pages offset it. A contains-URL query drilldown also showed “how to track calories” declining from 181,774 to 10,066 impressions with zero clicks in both periods. The contains filter includes related URL variants, so that query table is not an exact-URL-only export. The unusually large zero-click visibility should not be equated with lost customers, nor attributed to bots/AI without further evidence.
4. **Link diversity is limited in GSC's sample:** 242 reported external links, 221 from apple.com. Home and privacy receive 237 combined. This is largely App Store infrastructure rather than broad editorial endorsement of articles. Build useful resources people will reference; no evidence here warrants disavowing links.

GSC clicks do not establish app installs, subscriptions, revenue or newsletter conversions. CTA URLs exist and include tracking parameters, but GA/App Store conversion events were outside this audit.

- Manual actions and Security issues: both “No issues detected.”
- Crawl stats: 2,287 requests in 90 days, average response 241 ms. Both hosts report “No problems.” Responses: 86% 200, 9% 404, 5% 301. Purpose: 95% refresh, 5% discovery.
- 404 drilldown: 210 requests; recent examples include old hashed `/_next/static/css/` files, `/favicon.ico`, and `/app-ads.txt`. The sampled live article referenced a different current CSS hash. Check asset retention/cache consistency across deployments and provide a conventional favicon if desired. ## Content findings and priorities

**First: close the indexing follow-up loop.** Validate the now-working historical redirect. For materially improved clean articles, run a live Google test and request indexing once. Start with daily calories, high-protein meals, the diet plan and weight fluctuations because they support existing successful content. Keep sitemap URLs canonical and use honest modification dates. Repeated requests do not speed crawling, and requests do not guarantee indexing. [Google recrawl guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl).

**Second: improve evidence and usefulness.** The live daily-calorie article uses “CalorieCue Team,” has no linked research sources in its article body, and states specific statistics such as “80% ... quit within two weeks” without a citation. The belly-fat and beginner meal-prep bodies also lack external sources in local MDX. Verify, cite or remove unsupported numerical claims. Add accurate author information and qualified review for health guidance where available; do not invent credentials. Google's guidance emphasizes clear authorship, evidence and trust, especially for health topics. [Helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

The photo-logging article contains about 725 body words in local MDX. Length itself is not the issue: it describes CNNs, bounding boxes, training data, retraining and benchmark accuracy without demonstrating these are CalorieCue's actual implementation. Verify product claims and show original food-photo examples, estimates versus measured portions, correction steps and limitations.

**Third: strengthen the right internal links.** Weight fluctuations and metabolism each have one incoming MDX article reference; belly fat, fast weight loss and fasting each have two. Add links where they answer a natural follow-up question. Daily calories already has 28 such references, so more links alone are not a complete explanation or remedy for that page.

**Fourth: protect and improve search winners.** Prioritize the food list and calories-per-gram click-through opportunities, then recover the protein-ratio query. Continue improving the printable resource and free-app comparison. Review overlap among track/count/start-counting articles and among calorie-needs/TDEE guides, but merge only when query and content evidence establishes duplicate intent.

**Fifth: investigate deployment asset 404s.** Verify current HTML references only reachable CSS/JS and inspect cache/deployment behavior before deciding whether retention is necessary. Treat favicon cleanup as low priority.

Success measures: clean target articles becoming indexed, updated crawl dates, clicks and CTR for priority queries at comparable positions, and separately verified app/lead conversions. Do not use “zero excluded URLs” as the target.
